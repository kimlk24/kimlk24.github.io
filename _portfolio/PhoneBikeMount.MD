# The problem

Generic phone bike mounts either fit one phone size or one handle diameter well and compromise everywhere else. This one needed to fit the iPhone 12 mini specifically, clamp onto a range of common handle diameters, and switch between horizontal and vertical viewing without redesigning the mount.

# Approach

I modeled the whole assembly top-down: every part was created and edited directly inside the same assembly, positioned in its exact place relative to the phone and handlebar, instead of designing each part in isolation and hoping they would fit on assembly. That let the design account for fit before anything was printed. The phone holder uses four stems anchored to each corner, with a cutout for the phone's raised camera bump so it sits flush. A friction joint, tensioned by a single bolt, lets the holder rotate from horizontal to vertical and holds it at either orientation. The handle mount clamps around the bike handle with two bolts, accommodating a range of handle thicknesses. I printed the final parts in PETG for its heat resistance, so the mount would not deform on hot days or fatigue from road vibration.

# Results

* Designed and printed a working bike phone holder in **a single iteration**, because top-down modeling guaranteed the fit before anything was printed
